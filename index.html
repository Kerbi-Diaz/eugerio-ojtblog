Week 1

During the first week of my On-the-Job Training (OJT), we attended an orientation led by Ma’am Laarni. She explained the rules, expectations, and workflow of the office, which helped us understand how the organization operates. After the orientation, we were given a tour of the different departments in the DepEd office. Once the tour was finished, each intern was assigned to a department based on operational needs.

Initially, my fellow interns Trexxie, Kathlyn, and I were assigned to the ICT Office. However, in the afternoon, I was reassigned to the ASDS Office by Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo, the Assistant Schools Division Superintendent (ASDS). My first day in the ASDS Office was very hectic, as I was immediately introduced to Sir Fire, Ma’am Alyssa, and Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo.

The workflow in the ASDS Office is organized around weekly team goals. For that particular week, our main task was to sort and review official documents. This included checking files for incorrect spelling of signatories’ names. If any errors were found, the documents were returned to the Records Section to be sent back to the client or forwarded to the Accounting Office for proper signing. We also organized files by month, specifically for OPCRF and IPCRF documentation.

Although the tasks were demanding, the experience gave me an early understanding of how teamwork, attention to detail, and proper document management are essential in a professional office setting. It was a challenging but valuable start to my OJT experience.

Week 2

During the second week of my OJT, my tasks became more focused on document processing and office workflow. On Monday, Sir Welfire and I prepared several sets of documents placed in megaboxes for Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo to review and sign. These documents included travel authorities and other priority files that required her signature, as she was scheduled to attend an official event in Masbate.

The documents placed in the megaboxes were organized based on urgency, while the remaining files were sorted by date. These included OPCRF and IPCRF documents, which were carefully checked for the correct spelling of Ma’am Cheryll’s name in the signatories section. After the documents were signed, they were forwarded to the appropriate offices, such as the Records Section, SDS Office, or CID Office.

Throughout the week, Sir Welfire also taught me how to use the Document Tracking System (DTS). I learned how to identify where a document is routed after being signed and how it moves through different departments. This experience helped me understand the importance of proper document handling, accuracy, and coordination within the office.

×

Week 3

During the third week of my OJT, I was involved in both technical support and document processing tasks. One of the key events of the week was assisting in the operation of OBS (Open Broadcaster Software) for the live streaming of an official program. This provided me with hands-on experience in managing live broadcasts and understanding the technical setup required.

Alongside the live streaming activities, I processed documents that were returned from Masbate and had already been signed by Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo. These documents were sorted, reviewed, and prepared for distribution to the appropriate offices after verification.

Some documents returned from Masbate were no longer attached to their original folders or envelopes that contained the tracking slip. These papers were likely separated during transport or the signing process. To ensure proper tracking, the documents were re-encoded and reattached a new tracking slip, allowing clients and offices to continue monitoring their status even if the documents were separated from the original batch or attachment.

During the week, Sir Welfire and Ma’am Alyssa asked if I could create an overlay application to help streamline the DTS process. The idea was to make adding remarks quicker when scanning each document’s tracking slip. I explained that I would try to develop it, but it would be a learning curve for me since it was my first time creating a web store extension. This task allowed me to contribute to improving workflow efficiency while gaining hands-on experience with application development.

Week 4

During the fourth week of my OJT, I was assigned to technical tasks related to hardware troubleshooting and system setup. One of the main tasks involved fixing a laptop that encountered operating system issues caused by a faulty Windows update. I assisted in diagnosing the problem and restoring the system to proper working condition.

I also worked on assembling two system units intended for a school. This included installing new RAM and storage components, ensuring proper hardware configuration, and preparing the units for operating system installation.

After assembling the system units, I personally installed the Windows operating system and configured the devices for use. Meanwhile, my internmate assisted with photocopying and organizing documents. These tasks allowed me to strengthen my skills in hardware assembly, OS installation, and system preparation.

×

Week 5

During the fifth week of my OJT, most of my tasks focused on data encoding and record accuracy. I was assigned to encode CAR-RQA scores of teachers from examinations and assessments into the DepEd system, which required careful attention to detail to ensure that all entries were accurate and complete.

In addition to encoding, I also assisted in sorting related documents and reviewing records connected to the encoded data. This helped ensure consistency between physical documents and digital records within the office system.

I also handled documents from a previous batch that encountered issues during transportation and the signing process, which caused some files to be separated from their original tracking slip. These documents were verified and reattached with the correct tracking slip to restore proper tracking and ensure that they could be monitored throughout the remaining transaction process.

×

Week 6

During the sixth week of my OJT, I worked on a combination of technical and administrative tasks. One of the main activities involved salvaging usable components such as RAM from old or defective computers. These components were reused to upgrade system units with outdated CPUs and motherboards that were no longer suitable for current office workloads.

Due to the number of system units that needed upgrading, I worked together with Sir Robert and Sir John from the ICT Office. We transferred the salvaged components to better CPU and motherboard combinations to improve system performance and ensure the computers were suitable for daily office use.

I also assisted in improving the office environment by helping install decorative signage for the ASDS Office alongside Sir Welfire. This included placing the DepEd and ASDS office logos, which were made of plastic lettering with backlighting, as ordered by Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo to enhance the office’s visual presentation.

In addition to the technical tasks, I continued encoding CAR-RQA records and performed double-checking by comparing soft copies and hard copies with system entries. This process ensured data accuracy and consistency across physical and digital records.

×

Week 7

During the seventh week of my OJT, I was tasked with sorting and prioritizing documents that required immediate signature. These documents were organized and prepared for forwarding to the appropriate offices, including the Records Section, CID Office, SDS Office, Accounting, Budget, and Human Resources. I also used the Document Tracking System (DTS) to add remarks indicating the purpose of each document, its destination office, and updates for client tracking.

Part of my responsibilities included ensuring that documents were properly routed and delivered after processing. This task emphasized accuracy, proper coordination, and timeliness in handling priority documents within the office workflow.

This week also coincided with the Bida ang Guro Bazaar 2025, an event featuring booth displays from all 14 municipalities. Each booth showcased school initiatives, products, and services, and the exhibits were displayed throughout the week. The event was part of DepEd Sorsogon Province’s ongoing celebrations and activities recognizing teachers and schools.

I assisted Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo, together with Sir Welfire, during visits to each bazaar booth. As the Assistant Schools Division Superintendent (ASDS), Ma’am Cheryll served as one of the judges for booth evaluations focusing on design, creativity, organization, and overall presentation. Observing the evaluation process, alongside Sir Welfire and seeing Sir Jose (SDS) visit the booths, gave me firsthand exposure to how official assessments and fair judgment are conducted during large-scale DepEd events.

Week 8

The eighth week of my OJT was especially eventful as it coincided with the culminating activities of the month-long celebration of the National Teachers’ Month and World Teachers’ Day 2025 of SDO Sorsogon Province. The celebration resulted in a significant increase in office transactions and coordination requirements.

During the week, many teachers and clients visited the office to submit documents for Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo’s signature or to inquire about the status of previously submitted files. Some documents were newly endorsed, while others were being followed up to confirm whether they had already been signed or processed. Managing these requests was hectic due to the volume, but it helped me develop stronger skills in prioritization, organization, and handling multiple tasks under pressure.

In addition to document management, I assisted in preparing appearance certificates for activities related to the celebration and other upcoming events. This included cutting, organizing, and preparing the certificates for distribution. These tasks provided practical experience in administrative support during major institutional events and official office operations.

Week 9

During the ninth week of my OJT, I successfully developed the requested overlay application for the DTS process. I built it using existing applications, GitHub resources, and freely available code as references. After testing, I deployed the application as a web store extension, and it was used by Sir Welfire, Ma’am Alyssa, and other offices that rely on the DTS system. I also started using the application myself during DTS scanning for the week, which helped me see its practical benefits in real-time and ensured the workflow ran more efficiently.

In addition to deploying the application, I assisted in sorting OPCRF and IPCRF documents for signing, organizing them by date to streamline the workflow for Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo signing.

Toward the end of the week, Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo requested that I create a prototype web application for managing Accomplishment Reports and monthly plans. I informed her that I would try to develop it, as this would be my first time creating such an application, and I will be guided by Sir John from the ICT Office throughout the development. This provides a great opportunity to apply my learnings from school, explore web development, and contribute to improving office processes through digital solutions.

Week 10

The tenth week of my OJT became more eventful with the Kasanggayahan celebration parade. Together with Sir Welfire, Sir Lance, and the rest of the personnel from the DepEd Division Office of Sorsogon, I participated in the departmental activities related to the event. This experience allowed me to observe coordination and participation at the division level during a major provincial celebration.

During the same week, I handled documents left by teachers and clients that required processing through the Document Tracking System (DTS). Some of these documents needed urgent signing due to specific deadlines and immediate concerns. I also encountered files from previous weeks that were missing their DTS attachments, which required re-encoding and proper tagging to ensure they could still be tracked accurately within the system.

To help streamline the signing process, I was tasked with adding tabs and organizing documents in a way that made them easier for Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo to review and sign efficiently. This task emphasized the importance of document organization in supporting executive-level workflows.

In addition, I continued working on the initial prototyping and flow planning of the proposed application. This involved outlining how the system would function and how it could potentially improve existing office processes. Although still in the early stages, this task allowed me to apply analytical thinking and gradually build confidence in system design concepts.

Week 11

The eleventh week of my OJT was relatively lighter compared to previous weeks. There were fewer documents entering the ASDS Office, as most transactions during this period involved forwarding documents from our office to other departments.

My primary task for the week focused on scanning documents and encoding them into the Document Tracking System (DTS). After scanning, the documents were forwarded to the appropriate offices, ensuring that each file was properly recorded and routed according to its purpose.

Although the workload was lighter, this week emphasized consistency, accuracy, and proper coordination in routine office operations. It reinforced the importance of maintaining efficiency and attention to detail even during less demanding periods.

Week 12

During the twelfth week of my OJT, I assisted with restocking printer inks and organizing supplies in the office cabinets. I also refilled and maintained printers in both the ASDS Office and the BAC Office to ensure uninterrupted document printing and office operations.

In addition to maintenance tasks, I continued organizing and sorting documents that entered the office. Most of these files were processed through the Document Tracking System (DTS) and forwarded to the appropriate offices. This work required attention to detail and consistency to ensure documents were accurately recorded and routed efficiently.

Although much of the week involved routine tasks, these responsibilities reinforced the importance of organization, resource management, and timely handling of documents in supporting overall office workflow.

Week 13

During the thirteenth week of my OJT, I focused on organizing and preparing documents that required Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo’s signature. The files were arranged to support a smoother workflow, making the review and signing process more efficient and minimizing delays. During the seminar held at Villa Isabel Function Hall, I was assigned to assist Ma’am Cheryll Bermudo with the signing of priority documents, which required my full attention and prevented photo documentation of the activity.
